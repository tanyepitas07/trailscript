Four routes, tested rather than assumed, on 25 August 2026. Permission from AllTrails is in hand — so what follows is only about mechanism, not about whether we may.

C · A one-click bookmarklet

Tested, works, recommended first

A small button the clinician drags to their bookmarks bar once. On any AllTrails page they click it, and it reads what the page already publishes about itself and hands it straight back to TrailScript. It runs inside their own session, so there is no cross-site restriction and no bot challenge — the browser considers it the person's own script on the person's own page.

EffortA day
Data returnedName, difficulty, length, rating, review count, time estimate
Clinician costOne click

Two problems route C does not solve

Worth deciding before I build it, not after.

Matching names is genuinely hard

OpenStreetMap calls it Hulda Crooks Park. AllTrails calls it Razor Ridge to Jedi Trail to Beaumont Ridge Trail Loop. These are not the same object and often never will be — AllTrails indexes routes, OpenStreetMap indexes places. Merging them by name will produce confident wrong pairings.

My proposal: don't merge. Show AllTrails routes as their own clearly-labelled section — additional options, not enrichment of existing ones — and only attach AllTrails detail to an OSM site when the names match exactly. Everything else stays separate and honest.

These are hill trails, and your patients are urban

Every top AllTrails result near Loma Linda is a ridge climb of 2.5 to 6.4 miles. You told me this project is urban and urban-adjacent, for patients going somewhere after work. AllTrails' idea of a trail and your idea of a prescription may not overlap much, and its rankings will pull the tool toward exactly the places you said patients won't drive to.

My proposal: AllTrails data is shown, never used for ranking. Distance from the patient stays the sort order.

What I'd do

Build C, send the email for D. The bookmarklet works today, costs the clinician one click, and needs nobody's approval. In parallel, one email asking for a data export or API key turns the permission you already hold into route D — the fully automatic version you asked for. If they say yes, C becomes the fallback rather than the plan.
